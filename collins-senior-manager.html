I’ve read the req. Rather than walk you through my résumé, I want to take each thing it asks for and show you where I’ve already done it — most of it here, on these programs. Then one thing it doesn’t ask for.

“delivering on cost, schedule, scope, and quality commitments”

A 24-month estimate, delivered in 18.

The IMS-3500 / Aircraft Information Manager phase. It carried a company-wide Rockwell Collins delivery goal that fed the corporate Incentive Pay Program — so the date wasn’t mine, it was everybody’s bonus. The mechanism was an Agile-tailored Software Development Plan (SDP) I authored, replacing rigid phase-gate execution with iterative delivery. Speed did not come out of process: that software was classified DAL E and we developed it to DAL D rigor anyway — above what its classification required.

“provide technical leadership” · “communicate… to audiences with varying degrees of understanding”
AIM / IMS-3500 · Rockwell Collins, 2004–2013

The hardest team I ever led,
I had no authority over.

The management chain above me had fallen apart, and the company delivery goal was still on the books. So I drove out to the airport, sat down with Rockwell Collins’ own FAA representatives, and asked them point blank: we need to get this onto a customer aircraft — what do we need to do? We built the plan together. Then I checked in constantly, both with them and with the team, to keep everyone in sync. It shipped on time. You’re hiring for twenty reports. That story is the version where I had authority over none of them.

Connect, Count, Capable and Courage are Bettner and Lew’s Crucial C’s. Choice is my addition.

Flight Management Systems, 2019–2025 — teams of about eleven, ranging eleven to fifteen. I was handed engineering teams that weren’t working and asked to make them healthy. They were underperforming against their own capacity — and capacity was never what was missing. The five C’s are how I set about it, and I acted as the primary shield while it took hold: stabilising requirements with stakeholders and absorbing upstream dependencies, so the team could hold a roadmap instead of re-planning around churn. Earlier, on AIM / IMS-3500, I led the distributed test team in Chennai and won a worldwide Rockwell Collins award for excellence in communication for that coordination. The req mentions trips to India to coordinate between teams — I’ve been doing that work since before it was in a job description.

“lead and coordinate program-level cost estimates during the bid and proposal cycle” · “oversee and operate within budgets”

I’ve carried the number,
not just the schedule.

I’m drawing the distinction deliberately, because it’s the kind of thing that gets rounded up on résumés. The program was around $25M in total scope — that’s context, not a portfolio I personally managed. What I held as Control Account Manager was $10M+, running EVMS, holding budget and schedule variance under 5%. On the proposal side I worked customer-facing bids on the AIM and eFlight pursuits, using SEER-SEM for cost modeling.

“drive the evolution of team processes, methods, skillsets, tools, and technology while supporting Collins’ process methodologies”

I wrote one of the first Agile-tailored
SDPs at Rockwell Collins.

At Rockwell Collins I served as the formal Lean Champion and authored one of the first Agile-tailored Software Development Plans there — the SDP for AIM / IMS-3500 that replaced phase-gate execution with iterative delivery, inside a certification regime. The req asks for someone to drive process evolution while supporting Collins’ process methodologies. I’m not coming in to learn them — I helped write them.

not in the req

The sixth thing.

Same drawing. Same geometry, vertex for vertex — only the labels changed. An untrusted side, a certified side, and exactly one authorised path across. That’s what I did on the AIM interface to the IMS-3500, and it’s what I build now: every work package carries a published Engineering SOP and a git-verifiable audit trail, and a gate at the tool boundary blocks any action outside an approved plan. Strip the word “AI” off it and you have a DO-178C posture — evidence under configuration control. Your engineers are probably already using these tools. The question is whether anyone in the building can put them under engineering control. I’ve shipped it: StrictLock, an eleven-module fail-closed governance suite, with its eslint plugin published on npm.
